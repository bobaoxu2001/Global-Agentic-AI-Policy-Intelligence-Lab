### 0:30–1:25 — Show the 6 August policy refresh

**Screen:** `/policy-refresh`
**Action:** Show the dated policy-refresh cards. Open the full downloadable note only if the interviewer asks for source detail.

### 1:25–2:05 — Make the executive call

**Screen:** `/executive-brief`
**Action:** Point to the decision banner, one risk, one opportunity, and the five gates.

> For a hypothetical customer-facing agent, I would not approve a global launch from this sample. I would approve reusable identity, authorization, disclosure, provenance, logging, approval, testing, incident-response, and redress work. Then I would require product-specific and market-specific classification. The opportunity is that one well-evidenced control layer can reduce duplicated engineering and enterprise adoption friction. The limit is that a control design is not proof of legal applicability or operating effectiveness.

### 2:05–3:05 — Trace one claim end to end

**Screen:** `/provisions/eu-gdpr%3Aart22`
**Action:** Point to the Article 22(1) text, confidence, fact/inference/recommendation blocks, K01/K04 mappings, and verification date.

> This page shows where judgment enters. The fact is the Article 22 right concerning decisions based solely on automated processing that produce legal or similarly significant effects. The inference is narrower: an agent may raise this question when it finalizes a consequential outcome without independent human judgment. Whether Article 22 applies, and whether human involvement is meaningful, depends on the facts. The recommendation is therefore a real decision point for relevant outcomes and a record of the human’s role. K01 and K04 turn that into an approval gate and an action log, but neither control alone establishes compliance. The legal text is source-traceable; the agentic application remains a medium-confidence analytical inference.
